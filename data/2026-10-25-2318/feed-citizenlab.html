]]> The Lawful Access Act’s threats to Canada’s national security https://citizenlab.ca/the-lawful-access-acts-threats-to-canadas-national-security/ Fri, 16 Oct 2026 15:10:49 +0000 https://citizenlab.ca/?p=89685 Kate Robertson warns that Bill C-22 poses risks to Canada's national security in this piece for The Globe and Mail.

The post The Lawful Access Act’s threats to Canada’s national security appeared first on The Citizen Lab.

]]> “The assumption by government officials that technology companies can find “safe ways” to weaken critical cybersecurity protections badly misreads the current threat landscape,” argues the Citizen Lab’s Kate Robertson in a new piece for The Globe & Mail.

She warns that, if passed, the controversial Bill C-22 (“Lawful Access Act”) would empower the government to require surveillance capabilities be built into any communications technology in Canada. Everything from smartphones, smartwatches, banking apps, health apps, video game platforms, and public WiFi or CCTV networks could be accessed by law enforcement under the new bill.

The Lawful Access Act has drawn heavy criticism from tech companies, cybersecurity experts, and international standards bodies for endangering Canadians’ cybersecurity and for violating constitutional standards.

]]> The Secrets of a US Spyware King https://citizenlab.ca/the-secrets-of-a-us-spyware-king/ Thu, 08 Oct 2026 13:42:47 +0000 https://citizenlab.ca/?p=89604 Senior researcher John Scott-Railton speaks to WIRED about the lack of oversight at spyware company Paragon Solutions.

The post The Secrets of a US Spyware King appeared first on The Citizen Lab.

]]> Paragon Solutions vowed not to sell its mobile spyware to governments with poor human rights records and to cut off customers who misuse its products. However, in 2025 researchers at the Citizen Lab confirmed that the spyware had been used to target Italian activists and journalists. In an exclusive interview with WIRED, Paragon’s CEO Andrew Boyd revealed the limits of the company’s promise: that it has no way to know if customers misuse its software, and it doesn’t have a “kill switch” to disable customers when there is misuse.

Speaking with WIRED, Citizen Lab’s senior researcher John Scott-Railton noted that Paragon has  “less oversight, less transparency, less contractual protection against abuses than NSO Group,” another spyware company that has faced criticism for its use against journalists, activists, and others. 

“It’s exactly the opposite of the picture that Paragon has painted for itself for years,” says Scott-Railton, “And it signals to lawmakers and regulators that the spyware industry cannot be trusted to self-regulate.”

]]> Toronto-Based VPN Provider Plans to Quit Canada Over Lawful-Access Bill https://citizenlab.ca/toronto-based-vpn-provider-plans-to-quit-canada-over-lawful-access-bill/ Tue, 06 Oct 2026 18:13:30 +0000 https://citizenlab.ca/?p=89593 Psiphon, a software company that helps internet users under oppressive regimes evade online censorship, plans to leave Canada if the lawful-access bill passes.

The post Toronto-Based VPN Provider Plans to Quit Canada Over Lawful-Access Bill appeared first on The Citizen Lab.

]]> Psiphon, a software company spun out of the Citizen Lab that helps internet users living under oppressive regimes evade online censorship, plans to leave Canada if the federal government’s lawful-access bill passes in its current form, reports The Globe and Mail.

If passed, Bill C-22 would require electronic service providers to make secret changes to their systems to provide police services and the Canadian Security Intelligence Service (CSIS) with surveillance and monitoring capabilities.

Psiphon was originally created as a Citizen Lab project in 2006, and became a private company soon afterward. It has 20 million monthly users worldwide that rely on the software to bypass online censorship in countries such as Iran, Myanmar, Russia, and China. 

Despite its Toronto roots, the company says it is prepared to move its operations out of Canada if Bill C-22 is passed, as it would be forced to compromise crucial safety features put in place to protect the privacy of its users. 

Citizen Lab director and founder Ron Deibert says that Bill C-22 “will make it impossible for a large number of invaluable privacy-preserving tools and applications from operating in Canada.”

The post Group of Bipartisan Lawmakers Ask US Government to Ban Several Hack-for-Hire Firms appeared first on The Citizen Lab.

]]> In 2020, the Citizen Lab uncovered a massive hack-for-hire operation called Dark Basin. The group targeted American nonprofits, including organizations working on #ExxonKnew and organizations working on net neutrality advocacy. We linked the operation to an Indian company called BellTroX InfoTech Services, and related entities. 

On September 9, a bipartisan group of U.S. lawmakers sent a letter to U.S. Secretary of Commerce Howard Lutnick, urging him to add three Indian companies, including BellTroX, to the economic sanctions list. 

The lawmakers have accused the firms of  “conducting targeted espionage against U.S. citizens, businesses and the lawyers representing them,” as well as engaging in global lawfare to “censor investigative reporting by prominent American media organizations.”

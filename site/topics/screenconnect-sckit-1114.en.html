ScreenConnect / SCKit: RMM+ empty-key condition and RelayTrap reverse spread to technician workstations

Microsoft published its analysis of SCKit and RelayTrap on 11/13. It separates 2 distinct attack surfaces: the reported server-side entry via RMM+ extensions (where versions 1.0.31 and 1.0.32 accepted an empty access key when none was configured) and the client-side RelayTrap path that delivers a payload from a compromised customer endpoint to a connecting technician workstation (blocked by the CVE-2026-84869 fix in client 26.6.5+). SCKit silently installs a separate client tied to attacker infrastructure and persists via scheduled tasks and registry Run entries.

What to check

Removing an unauthorized ScreenConnect client alone does not clear scheduled-task or registry Run persistence or invalidate hijacked web sessions. Verify every technician client is at 26.6.5+, keep affected RMM+ extensions disabled, and investigate both technician workstations and downstream customer endpoints to revoke sessions and remove persistence.

ABOUT / EDITORIAL POLICY

About this site and how to read its information.

Infosec Briefing is an independent research site organizing incidents, vulnerabilities, and mitigations from public primary sources.

Operator and contact

Operated and edited by YomiPlush (yomiplush). This site does not act on behalf of developers, government agencies, or affected companies in making official announcements.

Sources we review

We cross-check vendor advisories, official releases, public source code, JVN, JPCERT/CC, IPA, NVD, GitHub Advisory Database, and other sources. Secondary reporting is treated as a lead; important conditions and mitigations are checked against primary sources.

AI assists collection and organization. Sources, affected conditions, and uncertainties are stated and corrected as needed. Impact varies by deployment, so ultimately check official guidance and actual running versions.

Meaning of timestamps and counts

Each report's “cutoff time” defines the investigation boundary. It is not the page publication time or incident occurrence time. Actual retrieval may occur after the cutoff, and content from that retrieval time is stored.

We distinguish occurrence, detection, disclosure, database registration, revision, and retrieval. Same-day material whose publication time cannot be verified is separated as reference information. Vulnerability counts, advisory counts, and numbers of discussion points are not directly treated as counts of damaging incidents.

Verifying fixes and recovery

Source code fixes, availability of fixed releases, deployment to user environments, compromise investigations, and service recovery are distinct stages. Do not describe plans as completed; when the minimum fixed version is unknown, say so.

Corrections and original evidence

We retain records for each investigation time and provide links from new reports to previous records. We publish URLs, retrieval dates and times, SHA-256 hashes of stored content, and failed retrievals so they can be checked later. Rights to retrieved originals remain with their respective holders; we do not reinterpret them as covered by this site's own license.

For errors or unclear wording, include the source and relevant page andGitHub Issuesor notify us by email. Do not post credentials or undisclosed personal information publicly.

Research & Open Source

Infosec Briefing helps developers, operators and independent researchers inspect the conditions and evidence behind public security information. Dated reports, structured JSON and sourced motion diagrams aim to reduce confusion between database registrations, vulnerabilities and damaging incidents.

We distinguish facts supported by primary sources, statements attributed to organizations or researchers, and unverified conditions. Unanalyzed NVD records and unreviewed GHSAs do not establish exploitation or harm. Failed retrievals and unknown fixed versions remain explicit.

Code and original documentation are published under the MIT license. Retrieved third-party materials retain their owners’ rights. Researchers can independently inspect sources and counts using saved responses, manifests, indexes and verification scripts in the repository.

Research methodology

Collection: NVD publication and last-modification queries are collected separately over fixed UTC windows, with pagination. For example, snapshot-1847.py collects GHSA published and modified windows for reviewed, unreviewed and malware types, following next-page Link headers. Scripts support collection, indexing and hash verification; topic selection and primary-source interpretation are not fully automated.

Cross-referencing and classification: CVE and GHSA IDs, reference URLs, vendor advisories, releases and public code are compared, retaining differences in affected conditions and fix status. Identifier intersections prevent simply adding publication, modification, NVD and GHSA totals. Topics use delta, scope-complement and reference-only categories. These are editorial categories, not severity or confidence scores.

Timestamps and integrity: Disclosure, database registration, revision, retrieval, fix availability and restoration are kept distinct. JST displays and UTC windows are recorded, with attention to overlapping boundaries. SHA-256 conventions vary by manifest. The 10/27 18:47 main collection hashes decoded bodies, vendor records hash saved bytes, and discovery records hash both. The 10/29 independent evidence manifest hashes saved bytes, including compressed gzip files. Matching hashes establish stored-content integrity, not the truth of claims.

Limitations: Responses are retrieval-time revisions collected after cutoff; recollection may not recover historical bodies. API limits, failed requests, empty JavaScript shells, anti-bot pages, unknown publication times, conflicting sources and historical rows not revalidated remain limitations. This is not a comprehensive incident census; omission or absence from KEV does not establish safety.
